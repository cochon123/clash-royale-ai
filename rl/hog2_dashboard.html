Method and limitations

These are local simulations in the recovered original native engine, played against frozen bot policies, not humans. Win rates here are not ladder win rates. The learner sees both deck lists, its own authoritative hand, both players’ elixir, and causal accepted-play history. It sees no unit positions, tower HP, images, or opponent hand, which limits how well it can react to the board. Hog1 may never have trained on some of the decks it controls as an opponent, so those opponents may be weaker than real players of those decks. Opponent-specific results have wide confidence intervals. A short training run with sparse terminal rewards may show little or no real improvement, and that is a legitimate result. “Best” was chosen on one seed set and is reported on a separate one. Matches that hit engine errors are excluded and counted.
Evaluation design, uncertainty, and run advice

The headline is the paired, macro-averaged score delta against the parent over the reported evaluation cells. A draw counts as half a win. Training win rates are sampler-skewed and shown separately. Per-matchup score intervals use the trainer-reported Hoeffding bounds; the headline interval is the trainer-reported paired bootstrap range. The page does not infer intervals. A pooled sample near 400 pairs has rough uncertainty around ±5 percentage points; 50 pairs in one cell is closer to ±14 points. These are approximate guidance, not a significance test.

This run uses its declared initialization, observations, evaluation set, and checkpointing plan. Current run changes and notes appear above when the trainer reports them. The policy model is initialized from the Hog1 4.4.2 checkpoint; the recovered original native engine is a separate component. The observations omit positions and tower health; claims about tower damage or board awareness are unsupported by this engine contract.
