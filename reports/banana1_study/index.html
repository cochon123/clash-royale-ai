Useful memory. No verified tower tracker.

The strongest evidence of learned memory is the location and side of recent deployments. Much of the impressive-looking threat and clock decoding is easier to explain: those features are already supplied to the model.

What this establishes

Banana-1's adapter reads accepted card plays, decks, hands, elixir and the clock. It does not read tower HP, crowns, tower existence, or troop entities. Adding or changing those fields cannot directly change its representation.

What remains open

A history-based estimate of damage or tower destruction could still be encoded. Proving that requires reliable HP labels aligned to the exact history and many independent games. We found no verified, replay-aligned board labels in the annotation queue.

HP accuracy: not measured. Tower-down accuracy: not measured. No neuron is designated a tower-HP neuron by this study. These are unknowns, not measured zero accuracy.
Why the saved tower recordings were not turned into a flattering accuracy score

Some logs contain clock resets from appended sessions; their current manifests cannot establish the deck and controller for every earlier session. Other clips are short, and a small number of long clips cannot support a convincing independent train/validation/test population. Hundreds of neighboring frames would mostly duplicate the same game state. Missing tower entities can also be a capture artifact; absence was never promoted to a destruction label.

03 / WHAT WAS OPENED

An action-history policy, with six places to look.

Last 64 events28 numeric features + 48-dimensional card embeddings → projection → two 160-unit GRU layers
→
Three branches meetGRU memory + 160-unit deck encoding + 160-unit global encoding → 160-unit fused state
→
3 refinement stepsSame 160-unit state refined three times → card scores, timing, and selected-card placement

The six measured vectors are GRU layer 0, GRU layer 1, global branch, deck branch, fusion before refinement, and fusion after refinement. Each has 160 coordinates. These 960 coordinates are readout locations, not all the neurons in the model. The two fused vectors are successive representations of the same-width state.

Supplied explicitly

Clock; reconstructed elixir; spending summaries; historical lane fractions; recently played threat flags; decks; and heuristic cycle features. In native play, current hand and both elixirs are replaced with engine readings.

Not observed

Current tower HP and loss; troop health and death; targeting; pathing; live unit locations; damage events. A recent Hog play is not a live Hog observation.

Banana-1 is an unspecialized behavior-cloning actor. Its export has zero PPO updates. The fresh critic and wait head are not learned game-state estimators and were excluded from the representation probes.

04 / THE ACCURACY ATLAS

How accurately can we read each feature?

These are test scores of new, small linear readers trained on frozen activations. They measure accessibility of information. They do not measure whether Banana-1 can act optimally on it.

Numeric entries: R², where 1 is perfect and 0 is the test-set mean predictor. Yes/no entries: balanced accuracy, where 50% is chance. These two score types should not be ranked against one another. “Single” is the best one-coordinate reader, with its layer and coordinate selected using validation only. 09 / HOW THE STUDY WAS DONE

Frozen actor. Separate probe battles. Explicit controls.

  1. Identity before interpretation. Loaded the Banana-1 export and the original v4.4.2 checkpoint. Compared every actor state tensor exactly. Checked the export checksum again after all experiments.
  2. Use existing data. Selected 1,500 available battles from the actor's legacy validation manifest using seed 20261023. Excluded its train IDs and final-test IDs. Removed identical side-canonical card trajectories; none were found in the candidate pool. No live games or simulated matches were launched.
  3. Split by battle. 900 battles train the readers, 300 choose hyperparameters and candidate single coordinates, and 300 supply the reported test results. Both sides of a game stay together. These are probe holdouts, not an untouched benchmark for the original actor; its warm-start ancestry is incomplete.
  4. Sample observations. At most 24 targets per battle, with both winning and losing perspectives allowed. Require at least eight prior events. Observe at the previous confirmed event, never at the target time. Exclude samples whose preceding event shares the target timestamp. Keep the native adapter's full known decks. This is event-boundary analysis, not a continuously evaluated live agent.
  5. Freeze and record. Run eval mode, float32, with three refinement steps. Capture the six 160-coordinate vectors. Initialize two identical architectures from seeds 17 and 83 for random-network controls; they never train.
  6. Fit low-capacity readers. Standardize each coordinate using probe training data only. Fit ridge regression with α ∈ {1, 10, 100, 1000}; select α using validation R² or balanced accuracy. Binary tasks use inverse-frequency class weights and threshold 0.5. This is a linear score reader, not logistic probability estimation.
  7. Search individual coordinates fairly. Fit one-dimensional readers from training data, with ridge term 1, then select among 160 coordinates per layer using validation. Select the winning layer on validation too. All reported performance is measured on the 300 test battles. Population curves use training-only marginal correlation rankings.
  8. Use controls. Read the raw globals directly; read only the clock; read two fully random networks; fit a final-state reader with permuted training labels and keep validation/test labels intact. That last check is a simple permutation sanity check, not an exact implementation of Hewitt and Liang's type-consistent control tasks.
  9. Quantify uncertainty. 300 whole-battle bootstrap replicates for final-state scores and trained-minus-random differences. Frames or observations are never treated as independent resampling units. Intervals exclude probe-refitting uncertainty.
  10. Test decision dependence. Clamp final-state coordinates ranked on training correlations. Use mean replacement, original decision heads, 1/4/16/32 coordinates, and eight random groups per size. Record card and tile argmax changes, card-distribution Jensen–Shannon divergence, and timing changes. This is an activation intervention, not a game-state counterfactual.
Definitions and formulas in plain language

R² = 1 − squared prediction error / squared error of the test mean. A negative value is worse than predicting that mean. MAE is average absolute error in the displayed units. Balanced accuracy = (sensitivity + specificity) / 2, so a classifier that always says “no Hog” scores 50%, even when Hogs are rare. AUC measures ordering of positive versus negative examples; it does not evaluate the fixed threshold. Jensen–Shannon divergence measures how far two card distributions move; zero means identical. We report it in natural-log units.

For a numeric concept: reader(h) = intercept + Σ coefficient[j] × (h[j] − training_mean[j]) / training_std[j]. The actor itself never uses these fitted reader weights.

Compute actually used

No actor training and no new game execution. The measured runtime covers extraction, fitting and interventions; report assembly and manual review are additional.

10 / WHAT THE RESULTS DO NOT SAY

A memory of deployment is not a model of combat.

No tower or troop accuracy claim

We did not measure HP, tower destruction, live troop count, survival, targeting, damage, or pathing. The label audit is part of the result: the current evidence cannot support those scores.

No human-level understanding claim

A high score can reflect supplied features, correlations with time, or the reader's fitting. A feature need not be represented as a clean symbolic variable or dedicated neuron.

Deployment differs from these replay probes

Replay elixir and hand are reconstructed, whereas the native adapter injects current engine elixir and hand. Both decks are known here. These probe errors cannot be presented as live elixir-tracking errors.

Descriptive, not pristine evaluation

The legacy validation set has already influenced original model development, and warm-start lineage is incomplete. The new probe test partition is separate for this study, but not newly collected actor data. Player/deck overlap and strategic correlations can remain.

A causal claim needs a specific semantic intervention with matched controls and an effect on behavior relevant to that concept. These mean ablations are a first dependency test. They are not a complete mechanistic explanation.
11 / LESSONS & NEXT EXPERIMENTS

Where the next useful evidence comes from.

  1. Label tower state before searching for tower neurons. Recover unambiguous existing session histories; align native six-tower snapshots or manually verified recorded frames with observation indices. Preserve destroyed towers at zero only when independently verified. Require many independent games with positive and negative cases in each split.
  2. Ask whether history helps beyond simple proxies. For HP, compare time-only, elapsed spend, lane pressure, deck and a small raw-history baseline against frozen activations. Include time/phase-matched comparisons so “late game” is not mistaken for “tower down.” Report MAE, R², balanced accuracy, AUC, calibration and prevalence as appropriate.
  3. Separate supplied from inferred features. Elixir and threat flags already reach the network. To study inference, remove these shortcuts consistently and compare models or representations under a declared protocol. An incoherent zeroed input is only a sensitivity check.
  4. Follow the recurrent location signal. Test the candidate GRU memory with matched left/right deployment pairs and causal readout interventions, while holding card, time, cost and perspective fixed. Confirm both directional behavior and specificity before naming a lane-memory circuit.
  5. Use the result to guide representation design. The final fused state makes some supplied numeric details less linearly accessible than random networks do, while preserving useful action-history clues. A policy need not maintain an accurate, complete simulator internally to imitate moves.
