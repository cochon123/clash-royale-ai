The problem this clock is for

Policy rollouts used to flip sides every turn: team, opponent, team, opponent… Real games do not. Same-side double-taps happen about 33% of the time. The clock's job is to predict whether the next action stays on the same side, and how many seconds until it lands.

Lessons learned

  • Rollout_policy_battles hardcodes next_side flip; any initiative signal must live outside the policy heads.
  • Labels are SAME vs OPPONENT relative to the side that just acted — not absolute team/opponent classification.
  • Delay target is log1p(Δt); report MAE in real seconds after expm1.
  • Collapse rate = fraction of rollouts with realism P(real) < 0.25.
  • Offline only: passing this experiment does not justify live play.
  • Actor succeeded (+6.1pp) while delay failed (1.74s MAE): initiative and timing should be gated separately.

Partial win: initiative is real (+≥5pp over alternation) — replace hardcoded side flip with the actor clock in rollouts. Delay misses the 1.25s MAE gate (only a small edge over phase-median); keep policy timing or phase medians until cycle/affordability features improve Δt.
