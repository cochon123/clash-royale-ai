THE CONTROL MATTERS

Does the new supervision help?

Both arms start from exactly the same Banana-1 weights and see the same human-action batches. The enriched arm also predicts coarse simulated state from its causal event history. The BC-only arm tells us how much comes from simply training the parent for longer.

BC-only control

Winner-side human cards, placement and timing, plus a small penalty for drifting away from Banana-1’s card distribution.

Banana-2 candidate

The same imitation losses, with a low-weight auxiliary loss for tower health, elixir and coarse living-unit count and HP summaries.

Actual native boards are labels during training. The deployed actor still receives replay history, decks, estimated cycle features and the known observation clock. The auxiliary head is saved separately and is not required to play.

The source sweep exported 258,150 rows, mostly from short prefixes. This run uses only matching prefixes with at least eight prior events, the parent vocabulary, compatible physical orientation and a distinct query timestamp. It caps native supervision at twelve rows per battle; the final eligible count is shown above.

What this experiment can establish

A controlled imitation comparison

Training and development remain separated by whole battles. Side-swapped copies of held-out trajectories are excluded from training. The original final test stays reserved.

A useful internal prediction task

The native summary is queried at a known clock with only preceding confirmed actions as inputs. The pending card, its position and its timing label are discarded on the auxiliary path. No native hand or board values enter the actor.

Limits that remain after training

Original levels, evolutions, seed, initial hand and balance-version fidelity are unverified. Accepted engine actions do not prove that the reconstructed board matches the original match. The parent’s legacy development ancestry also prevents treating this as a pristine generalization test.

All source quarantine flags remain unchanged. A finished run produces a research candidate, not an automatically promoted replacement for Banana-1. A lower development loss alone does not prove better live play.
