00 / BET

The bottleneck is an incomplete
decision state.

There are two layers to this diagnosis: the measurement pipeline is unreliable in several important places, and the policy’s input/output contract does not describe the real decision it must make.

First: make the evidence trustworthy

Your code audit changes the starting line.

I incorporated all ten substantive issues you supplied, plus the maintainability concerns. A clean baseline must precede another expensive model run. Fixing the pipeline may lower reported accuracy while improving the model we actually deploy.

The experiments here deliberately avoid old rollout caches, legacy split helpers, shared vocabularies, and synthetic winner scores. They retain the frozen models and legacy event encoding to isolate specific effects; they do not constitute a repaired benchmark.

Then: change what is represented

Remember what the history implies now.

The current GRU compresses deployments into one vector. Its ordinary history/global features stay at the last event; the threat suffix is the main feature that advances with the query time. It has no tower HP or observed troop state.

Meanwhile, it predicts “the next action, given who will act.” A live controller must decide whether to act at all, on a changing board, with incomplete information. The architecture should learn that complete contract.

The recommendation: build Astra v8 — Causal Belief Policy in two stages. First test time-aware predictive memory and a joint action clock on corrected replay data. Then ground the same memory in sparse board observations. The second stage is my highest-upside route to a much stronger player; replay-only gains should be treated as bounded hypotheses.
01 / PAST

What the project has already learned.

Version numbers are branches, not a chronological leaderboard. The v7 pilot predates v4.4. The useful story is which hypotheses have survived scrutiny.

Legacy → cleaner → reproducible priors

The July investigation documented missing abilities, inferred decks/hands, duplicates, and an overfit 13.9M-parameter legacy model. The cleaner addresses important attribution problems, but the training loader still reparses raw files. That connection remains unfinished.

v2 / v3 / v4: cycle, response, placement

Per-card scoring, threat features, and card-conditioned placement established a useful action prior. Natural-hand defense audits were much more informative than forced counterfactual hands. The old exact-hand rescore moved overall card top-1 by only 0.0029 percentage points.

v4.1 / v4.2 / v4.3: data, mirrors, compute

More training data and a larger trunk produced modest reported improvements. The shared “think” MLP repeatedly transforms the same vector; it adds no observation or simulated consequence. Its archived K=0→8 card gain was only 0.76 points, on a slice whose lineage still needs auditing.

v4.4 / v4.4.1 / v4.4.2: distributions and selection

Heatmaps preserve multiple placement modes. v4.4.1 already conditions on the selected card and samples top-five bins; that is worth keeping, not relabeling as a new idea. v4.4.2 clones eventual winners, but a winner’s every move is not necessarily better. The fresh probe does not establish a v4.4.2 advantage.

v5: optimizing style

Style matching and offline REINFORCE optimize a human-likeness judge, not a game reward. Changing the rollout clock made that judge’s scores jump dramatically even for an unchanged v4.1. “Looks human” cannot serve as the main progress metric.

v6 / v7: the state hypothesis starts

v6 explored heatmaps; some “hide opponent” runs recorded a hide probability of zero. v7 added a 16-channel decaying deployment raster and a small residual adapter. Aligned memory did not clear the shuffled-control promotion gates. It remembered where actions happened, not which troops remained alive.

Auxiliary models and rollout autopsies

The action clock improved archived actor prediction from 67.1% to 73.2%, but missed its timing gate. Replacing XY revived all 27 collapsed realism seeds; those replacements used empirical test-corpus positions, so this diagnoses a judge sensitivity, not a deployable fix. Winner-based tournament and matchup rankings lack a validated gameplay interpretation.

The phone lab and tower data

Hand detection, confirmed deployments, elixir reconciliation, and HP snapshots create a route to grounded state. The audit found 579 frames across 10 games. That is useful for a perception pilot, but tiny and correlated compared with the replay corpus. No broad board-state learner has yet been trained here.

Known limits

A diagnostic cohort.

Whiskers: 95% battle-bootstrap intervals. Hover each bar for exact bounds. Orange uses the human card and is not deployable. “Neutral XY” changes history coordinate channels only; derived spatial globals remain.

Timing target leakage

A 2.77-point card gap.

For v4.4.2, replacing target-time threat ages with ages at the last observed event lowers card top-1 from 55.6% to 52.9%. Paired change: −2.77 pp [−3.37, −2.20]. Timing MAE rises by 0.302 s [0.282, 0.323].

2,058 of 8,201 samples change their threat inputs. In 1,490 samples, the recent win-condition age encodes the future delay algebraically: age × 5 seconds, minus the known prefix-to-threat gap. This is leakage for a post-prefix next-event forecast.

At a genuine live observation time, elapsed threat age is valid information. The problem is treating the future action time as that observation time while also predicting its delay. A frozen ablation measures dependence; it does not predict the result of retraining correctly.

Card / placement coupling

The correct card opens a 7.67-point gap.

Under causal/revealed inputs, v4.4.2 placement-bin top-1 goes from 14.69% to 22.36% if placement receives the demonstrated card: +7.67 pp [7.02, 8.28]. Top-five placement coverage rises from 36.63% to 51.54%.

The true card lies in the top three and its true placement lies in that card’s top five bins on 48.1% of samples. That is the observed coverage of a hypothetical 15-candidate menu, not the accuracy of a ranker. It motivates testing candidate selection; it does not prove we can rank those candidates correctly.

v4.3’s soft-card architecture ignores the explicit placement-slot override, so its oracle-card condition is not an oracle test. The visualization calls this out.

There is no fresh evidence that winner-only training wins. On the causal/revealed cohort, v4.4.1 achieves 52.94% card top-1 versus v4.4.2’s 52.87%. The paired difference, v4.4.2 minus v4.4.1, is −0.07 pp with a 95% interval of [−0.58, +0.51]. Prefer a clean all-actions baseline before adding outcome weighting.

Same-index reference baselines: uniform over the heuristic hand gives 24.50% expected card accuracy; prefix-frequency gives 11.95%. Those are measured on these exact examples. Placement-bin top-1 uses the model’s existing 32 X bins × 18 Y bins, not the game’s physical tile lattice. The old training report’s tile_acc is instead a Euclidean distance threshold. Its xy_mae is mean Euclidean distance, whereas the old blind-spot probe used L1 distance. Those differently named/scaled quantities must not be pooled.

04 / V8

Astra v8: a causal belief policy.

Keep the compact action prior and multimodal placement. Replace the single static decision vector with a small memory that evolves in time, receives observations, and serves both scheduling and action selection.

PROPOSED · CLICK A MODULE TO EXPLORE

Select a module for details. On small screens, scroll the diagram horizontally.

Memory with interactions, not just age.

Use a causal event encoder with relative time and spatial features, plus a fixed bank of 24 latent memory tokens. Each event updates the bank through cross-attention; a small interaction block lets friendly and enemy evidence influence one another. An elapsed-time transition advances memory between events.

Condition all action heads on this memory. In v7, a raster adapter only perturbed placement on a frozen trunk. Here memory can change which card, when, and where. Learned persistence and uncertainty must remain latent until actual observations supervise them.

Suggested starting sizes: 64 recent events · d=128 · 24 latents · 4 attention heads · 2 interaction blocks

Ground the state for the bigger gain.

Stage A predicts observable future events to learn a useful summary from replay histories. This reorganizes existing information; it cannot reveal an unobserved troop death with certainty. Stage B adds a lightweight board-observation adapter, initially from the stored frames and reviewed labels.

Start with HP and coarse troop presence by region, with confidence and missingness. Train offline on synchronized observations/actions; cache image features. Do not paint guessed troop trajectories into the training set as ground truth. An attention module by itself does not fix missing supervision.

Observation plan: six HP values + masks · region-level troop evidence · own elixir/hand · all timestamped
Why I expect a ceiling from replay-only work: two board situations can be indistinguishable under this policy’s observed event history and omitted metadata, yet require different reactions. Any deterministic history-only policy must act the same in both. A belief can represent the uncertainty; a board observation can resolve some of it. That is the central reason Stage B has higher upside than another trunk-size increase.

Research basis: neural point processes provide a model of event types and timing from an evolving hidden state; this motivates the clock design, not a promised Clash Royale result. Mei & Eisner, Neural Hawkes Process (2017). Recurrent latent dynamics with observation updates motivate the memory/observation separation. PlaNet itself learns from environment interaction; our proposed replay-first training is an adaptation, not a reproduction. Hafner et al., PlaNet (2019).

05 / TIME

Waiting belongs inside the model.

We have trained on what happened. We also need a likelihood for the time during which nothing happened.

One shared timeline. Two event risks.

ABBAAB
Own actionOpponent eventSilence is evidence

After each new observation, update both event-rate predictions. In a real match the opponent’s hazard is a forecast, not an action we execute. Only the controlled player’s action is sent to the device.

Illustration only. Recompute after an opponent event or new board observation; do not blindly replay a precomputed action sequence.

A coherent likelihood

p(next event at t, side s, action a | H)
= survival(t | H)
× event_rate(s, t | H)
× p(a | belief(t), s)

survival(t) = exp(−∫ Σ event_rate(s, u) du)

H contains only observations already available. The survival term rewards correctly predicting quiet intervals. The action likelihood separates ability activations from deployments; only deployments incur placement loss.

Implementation: piecewise-constant rates, evaluated on a 250 ms decision grid; integrate the time loss over intervals with correct duration weights. Preserve original 50 ms replay timestamps and group same-tick actions, rather than inventing an order. Include terminal censoring when the reliable observation window ends.

A joint action head with real candidates

Predict action type, then score each available card against memory. For each candidate card, generate a spatial distribution and a within-bin offset. Factorization remains useful, but never form a placement by averaging incompatible card embeddings or opposite-lane coordinates. Sample a coherent tuple or use a calibrated joint decoding rule. Use held-out NLL and joint accuracy to select the decoder; probability peaks alone are not strategic utility.

The old top-three-card / top-five-placement coverage suggests a 15-candidate reranking experiment. A supervised ranker can first learn agreement with supported human actions. A value-based planner is a later extension, after board observations and action consequences are validated. An event predictor can imitate what players do next without knowing what would happen if we chose differently.

A future advantage-weighted or offline-RL extension should remain close to supported actions. IQL is a relevant reference because it avoids explicit value queries on unseen actions during training, but it still assumes informative states, rewards, and dataset support. I would not put it behind our current full-game judge. Kostrikov et al., Implicit Q-Learning (2021).

What “massive” would mean

A large relative gain can still be weak play.

Raising joint action agreement from 11.9% to 20% is about a 68% relative improvement. It still matches one human card-and-bin target only one time in five. Several alternative moves may also be good, so this is neither a 20% chance of a good move nor a 20% win rate.

For a future real match comparison, 60% against the incumbent is a concrete target, not my forecast. Under a simple independent-binomial approximation, about 200 games gives roughly 80% power to detect 60% versus 50% at a two-sided 5% level. Deck/side pairing and correlation change that calculation.

Do not launch that campaign now. First exhaust stored data and offline falsification. A small eventual phone smoke test checks execution; it does not establish strength.

How I would interpret a result: Stage A may yield no material gain once the pipeline is fixed. If so, stop scaling it. The numbers above are an investment threshold, not a rationale for moving the goalposts. Stage B requires additional, diverse observational supervision; ten self-play games cannot support a broad win-rate claim.

Compute budget · proposal

Keep v8 small enough to falsify cheaply.

Start around 2–4M trainable parameters, d=128, 64-event chunks with 24 recurrent memory tokens. Use mixed precision, batch 128–256 if measured memory permits, a bounded stream cache, and cached visual embeddings. Run a throughput/peak-VRAM benchmark before estimating full-run time.

Target an inference budget below 50 ms p95 for policy/state on the requested RTX 3060 8GB; perception and phone latency are separate. This is a design budget, not a measured speed. Parameter-state storage alone is roughly 32–64 MB at 16 bytes/parameter; activations determine much of the real memory demand.

The machine inspected actually exposes an RTX 3050 Laptop GPU with 6GB, plus about 30GiB RAM. The frozen probe ran there with four Torch CPU threads. Future runs should fit this smaller device too, or explicitly target your 3060.

08 / OPTIONAL SCIENTIFIC MISCONDUCT BY THE METAPHOR DEPARTMENT

Astra's rambeling

I keep imagining our current model as a very diligent sports commentator who has been locked in a cupboard. Someone slides little notes under the door: “Hog, left lane, 12 seconds ago.” The commentator replies, with excellent grammar, “Cannon, probably.”

There is a real achievement in that cupboard. It has learned cycles, responses, placement habits, and some very elaborate ways of being statistically normal. I want to give it a clock, a memory that can revise itself, and eventually a window.

My favorite future debug screen would have a little thought bubble over each lane: “I suspect a surviving Mini P.E.K.K.A, but I haven’t seen him for six seconds and my confidence is falling.” That is a far more useful internal monologue than “the average human put something around here.”

And yes, I want it to learn the ancient, devastating Clash Royale technique of doing absolutely nothing for one second. A bold research agenda. Several million parameters, deployed in the service of keeping one’s thumb still.

♞

The council of questionable ideas

Give each uncertainty estimate a tiny helmet. A hypothesis is allowed to charge down the lane, but it should admit when it cannot see the bridge.

The idea I would actually fund: spend a fixed, tiny budget trying to prove the memory useless. If aligned observations consistently rescue decisions that shuffled observations cannot, we have earned the right to get excited.

The idea I would not fund yet: Monte Carlo tree search through a dream of an arena that has never been taught what damage looks like. That is a very expensive way to imagine winning.

This section is commentary, not experimental evidence. The horse has not passed validation.

Metric definitions, remaining uncertainty, and falsification
  • Card top-1: agreement with one recorded human card among eight deck slots. The next actor is supplied by the target. A legal alternative can still be scored wrong.
  • Placement-bin top-1/top-5: exact coverage on the legacy 576-bin head. The same bin definition is used across this probe. v4.3 bins its continuous prediction and has no top-five distribution.
  • Joint card+bin: both targets correct in the same example. No independence assumption or multiplication of marginal accuracies.
  • Oracle-card: the placement head receives the human card. Slot output is unchanged. Only selected-card architectures support this override.
  • Causal intervention: query-time threat features at the last confirmed event, with no target timestamp. Legacy cycle/global defects and heuristic legality remain. It is a frozen input-distribution shift, not a claim about causal retraining performance.
  • Revealed deck: unrevealed enemy cards are UNK, based on the entire prefix. This also introduces distribution shift for models trained with complete opponent decks. Its small marginal effect here does not eliminate opening-game uncertainty.
  • Data lineage: all available cached IDs and saved manifests were excluded. Exact normalized event/deck fingerprints were also excluded. Unknown historical caches, equivalent swapped representations, and missing ancestor manifests prevent an absolute non-membership proof.
  • Action uncertainty: the proposed latent memory is not guaranteed to identify real entities from behavior alone. Multi-horizon prediction must beat a same-budget control. Grounding needs observations sufficiently diverse to transfer beyond ten self-play games.
  • Win estimation: neither a style discriminator nor a predictor trained on completed human games validates action counterfactuals. Higher confidence does not repair the missing synthetic inputs or calibration shift.
  • Failure result: if cleaned baseline + clock matches the full memory architecture, keep the simpler model. If perception cannot produce reliable held-out state evidence, postpone planning rather than inventing HP rewards.
