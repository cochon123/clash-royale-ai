How the experiment works

Lessons learned

  • The realism scorer said policy rollouts look real (0.85 vs 0.87); against actual policy negatives the same feature set separates them perfectly. A discriminator is only as strong as its negatives.
  • Top tells: alt_rate, in_single, frac_triple, in_overtime, frac_gap_lt_1. These are what a policy must fix to become statistically human.
  • Several top tells come from the rollout harness (strict side alternation, fixed 40-event continuation), not the policy. Removing all 13 harness features still leaves AUC 1.000, so the style gap is real either way.
  • Detection is not a close call, so mean P(human) — not accuracy — is the ranking signal while every policy sits at AUC 1.0.
  • The judge needs roughly 8 generated actions before it is confident, so short bursts of policy play are much harder to spot than full continuations.
  • Dropping the 60 strongest tells still leaves near-perfect detection: the AI signature is distributed across the whole feature set, not one fixable bug.

What to fix next

  1. Fix the harness before blaming the policy: sample who acts next instead of forcing strict alternation, and vary continuation length so n_events stops being a constant.
  2. Then attack the strongest genuine tell, frac_gap_lt_1: human 0.172 vs AI 0.037 (2.9σ apart).
  3. Placement and timing are regressed to the conditional mean, which halves their spread versus humans. Sampling from the predicted distribution should close several tells at once.
  4. Re-run this judge after each policy change — it needs no live play and the rollout cache makes repeat runs cheap.
