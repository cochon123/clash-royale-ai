The autopsy: what kills a rollout

The policy continues 200 real battles for 40 actions each and the realism scorer rates the result. About one in seven comes out as garbage. This experiment re-runs every corpse five times, each time replacing one part of the policy with a human-like substitute, and asks which replacement brings it back to life.

How the autopsy works

1 200 seeds real battles truncated to 12 warm-up actions
2 Policy continues 40 sampled actions, temperature 0.8
3 Realism scorer judges collapse if P(real) < 0.25
4 27 collapses each re-run 5× with one piece replaced
5 First rescue wins score > 0.5 attributes the cause

Is the judge just paranoid?

Before blaming the policy, check the scorer. Real battles put through the same blender — truncated to the same length, forced into strict alternation, delays clipped — almost never collapse. The judge is reacting to the policy, not to the format.

Lessons learned

  • Collapse rate=13.5% (27/200); healthy=160 mid=13.
  • Single-cause attribution rate=100.0% → PASS (≥70% required).
  • OOD truncated-real mean=0.899, strict-alternation-real mean=0.866, clipped-delay-real mean=0.942.
  • Every collapse recovers under human_like_xy (universal), but first-wins attribution splits across causes (dominant policy_xy_head 48%). Mean realism gates are weak single-head signals — prefer head-wise metrics plus XY-focused checks.
  • Live play remains unjustified — this autopsy is offline only; v3 ready_for_live_smoke_test is treated as suspect under audit.
